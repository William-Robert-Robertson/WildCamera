No. If you would be able to aim your triggering device in case of a nest box or tree hole a little bit above and below the box or hole, then it would be triggered sooner while the animal approaches, and capture the whole animal entering or leaving. Yes - there are traditional wide angle PIR sensors that can be aligned horizontally or vertically - there are also the newest generation of PIR sensors which can be configured from software.

Having more than one PIR is no problem.

I've read about modern (available in the last 2 years) mm wave radar sensors - they consume more power but the manufacturers (TI and Infineon) claim that they're more flexible (for example, a minimum to maximum distance range can be set in configuration) - I haven't had a chance to test this yet though - in the marketing and technical documents the manufacturers talk about human detection not dormouse detection.
